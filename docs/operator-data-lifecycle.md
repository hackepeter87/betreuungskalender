# Operator data lifecycle and privacy roles

This guide helps operators record deployment-specific retention and erasure
decisions. It is not legal advice and does not provide generally valid legal
bases or retention periods. Complete the matrix for the actual installation
and have it reviewed before publishing legal information or processing an
erasure request.

The application currently supports soft deletion for many domain records. A
normal delete action hides a record from active views but can retain the record,
its references, and audit history in the selected operational database. The
application does not currently provide a selective GDPR erasure or
anonymization workflow. That future design is tracked in
[RFC #487](https://github.com/hackepeter87/betreuungskalender/issues/487). Do
not describe the current delete buttons as a complete erasure mechanism.

the same retention and restore-reconciliation process.

## Request workflow

Use this workflow for requests involving access information or erasure. Article
13 GDPR applies when personal data is obtained from the data subject; Article
14 can apply when data about a person is entered by somebody else. Article 17
erasure is not absolute and includes exceptions, including certain legal
obligations and the establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims.

1. Record the request date, scope, response deadline, and responsible reviewer
   without copying unnecessary personal content into operational logs.
2. Verify the requester's identity proportionately. Do not disclose whether an
   unrelated person or record exists before authorization is established.
3. Locate data across the live database, audit history, identity provider,
   push provider, external calendar provider, backups, reports, exports, and
   transfer copies controlled by the operator.
4. Review the purpose, legal basis, applicable Article 17 grounds and
   exceptions, preservation duties, other affected persons, and whether
   deletion or effective anonymization is appropriate for each category.
5. Document the approved action. Until #487 is implemented, use a reviewed
   operator procedure; ordinary application soft delete is not sufficient by
   itself where actual erasure or anonymization is required.
6. Revoke active access, sessions, invitations, feeds, and push subscriptions
   when their purpose has ended. Handle identity-provider records separately.
7. Apply the approved action to controlled live and exported copies. For
   immutable backup generations, prevent ordinary reuse, let the documented
   retention cycle expire them, and define how a later restore is reconciled.
8. Verify completion and retain only the minimum request record needed to show
   how the request was handled. Record any lawful restriction or refusal and
   the information supplied to the requester.

Do not promise immediate selective deletion from every backup if the backup
system cannot perform it safely. The operator policy should instead define
access restriction, generation expiry, restore reconciliation, and the actual
maximum period until all controlled backup generations expire.

## Controller and processor checklist

Self-hosting alone does not determine the legal role. Record the actual
relationship for each installation.

- **Controller:** Who determines purposes and means? Who supplies Articles 13
  and 14 information, handles rights requests, sets retention, and selects
  recipients and processors?
- **Joint controllers:** Do multiple parties jointly determine purposes or
  essential means? If so, obtain advice on the required allocation and
  transparent summary rather than assuming a processor relationship.
- **Processor:** Does one party process personal data only on documented
  instructions for a controller? If so, review Article 28 requirements,
  sub-processors, security measures, assistance, deletion/return, and audit
  rights for that specific relationship.
- **Independent recipients:** Identify identity, hosting, email, push,
  calendar, support, or professional-service providers and determine their
  actual role instead of assigning one label to every recipient.
- **International transfers:** Record any transfer outside the EEA and the
  applicable deployment-specific safeguards where relevant.

## Browser storage

Opaque session cookies are used only for authentication modes that establish
an application session. A separate recovery cookie is used only when the
recovery-admin feature is enabled and invoked. Local interface storage keeps
presentation preferences.
Optional service-worker cache storage starts only after accepted installation,
standalone use, or explicit push activation. No analytics or advertising
storage is included.

Section 25(2) TDDDG can remove the consent requirement when storage or access
is strictly necessary to provide a digital service explicitly requested by the
user. Apply that exception only to storage that meets its conditions. Describe
the storage and its duration in the operator privacy information; do not add a
general consent banner solely for the documented necessary storage. Optional or
future storage purposes require a separate assessment.

## Review record

Before publication, record:

- operator and installation scope;
- responsible reviewer and legal-review date;
- completed retention matrix and next review date;
- controller, joint-controller, processor, and recipient decisions;
- actual backup, log, export, and identity-provider locations;
- current application version and known technical limitations;
- approved Articles 13/14 information and rights-request contact route.

Primary legal references:

- [GDPR Articles 5, 13, 14, 17, and 28](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- [Section 25 TDDDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html)
